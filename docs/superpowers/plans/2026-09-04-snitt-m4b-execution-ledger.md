CRITICAL -- THE TIMELINE AND THE EDL ARE ON DIFFERENT CLOCKS, and it is exactly the kind of defect only whole-branch review can see: it lives in the COMPOSITION of two tasks, not in either one. updateNSView feeds the view `duration: controller.durationSeconds`, which CompositionBuilder sets to `cursor` -- the OUTPUT duration, the sum of kept ranges. But edl.cuts are consumed by KeptRanges.compute(duration: assetDuration) in SOURCE time. So after the first trim the view's axis is output time and every later drag appends an output-time range into a source-time list.
Verified by execution on an 8s bundle: first drag x0->400 cuts source 0-4, duration correctly 4.0. Second drag x400->600 on the same 800px view yields TimeRange(2,3) -- INSIDE the already-removed region -- and duration stays 4.0. The user drags and nothing happens, silently. That is the feature's second-most-common interaction.
Two secondary symptoms share the root: cuts are DRAWN in source time on an output-time axis (a 0-4 cut repaints as the whole bar), while jumpPoints and the polled playhead are correctly in output time. One bar mixing three coordinate spaces.
My ruling on the fix: put the timeline on the SOURCE clock. The deeper argument is that cutRects only MEANS anything on a source axis -- a cut by definition is absent from the output, so it has no output-time position. Drawing cuts on an output axis is incoherent, which is why this direction is right rather than merely convenient. The playhead and jump points then need the inverse mapping (output -> source), which belongs beside TimeRangeMapping.trimmedTime in SnittDocument with its own tests, not inlined in a view.
